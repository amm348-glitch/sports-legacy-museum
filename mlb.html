MLB
Baseball

America's pastime. Major League Baseball traces its professional roots to 1876 — making it the oldest major professional sports league in the United States, and a mirror for American history itself.

By the numbers: 30 Teams — 148 Seasons — 162 Games Per Season — $11B Annual Revenue
The Greats

Baseball Legends

The hitters, pitchers, and fielders who wrote the story of America's pastime across 150 years.

Second Base · Brooklyn Dodgers
Jackie Robinson

On April 15, 1947, Robinson broke MLB's color barrier. A 6× All-Star, 1949 batting champion, and 1947 Rookie of the Year — his courage on and off the field made him the most important figure in baseball history.

3
Outfield · New York Yankees
Babe Ruth

The Sultan of Swat. Ruth's 60 home runs in 1927 stood as the single-season record for 34 years. His 714 career home runs defined power hitting and made baseball America's dominant sport during the 1920s and 30s.

24
Center Field · New York Giants / Mets
Willie Mays

2× MVP, 2× World Series Champion. "The Say Hey Kid" is widely regarded as the greatest all-around baseball player ever. His 1954 World Series catch — known simply as "The Catch" — remains the most celebrated defensive play in baseball history.

44
Outfield · Atlanta Braves
Hank Aaron

Broke Babe Ruth's all-time home run record on April 8, 1974 — finishing with 755 career home runs. Aaron endured death threats and hate mail throughout his pursuit of Ruth's record, carrying himself with extraordinary dignity.

42
Pitcher · New York Yankees
Mariano Rivera

The greatest closer in baseball history. 5× World Series Champion, 13× All-Star. Rivera's cut fastball was so dominant that hitters knew exactly what was coming — and still couldn't hit it. First unanimous Hall of Fame inductee.

4
Outfield · Boston Red Sox
Ted Williams

The last player to hit .400 in a season (.406 in 1941). 2× MVP, 2× Triple Crown winner. Williams lost nearly five prime years to military service — making his career statistics all the more remarkable.

32
Pitcher · Los Angeles Dodgers
Sandy Koufax

4× no-hitter, including a perfect game. 3× Cy Young Award winner, 2× World Series Champion. Koufax's 1965 season — 26 wins, 382 strikeouts, 2.04 ERA — is considered one of the greatest pitching seasons in history.

21
Outfield · Pittsburgh Pirates
Roberto Clemente

2× World Series Champion, 1966 MVP, 12× Gold Glove. Clemente died in a plane crash on December 31, 1972, delivering aid to earthquake victims in Nicaragua. MLB retired his number league-wide and named its humanitarian award after him.

2
Shortstop · New York Yankees
Derek Jeter

5× World Series Champion, 14× All-Star, 5× Gold Glove. "The Captain" of the Yankees dynasty was the face of baseball in New York for two decades — defined by clutch performances and unwavering professionalism.

Defining Moments

The History of the Game

1876
National League Founded

The first major professional sports league in American history is established with eight charter teams.

1927
Ruth Hits 60 Home Runs

Babe Ruth's Yankees — "Murderers' Row" — post a 110-44 record. Ruth's 60 home runs stood as the record for 34 years.

1947
Jackie Robinson Breaks the Color Line

Robinson's debut with the Brooklyn Dodgers is one of the most significant moments in American sports and civil rights history.

1974
Aaron Breaks Ruth's Record

Hank Aaron hits his 715th home run on April 8, 1974 — breaking Babe Ruth's all-time record amid death threats and hate mail.

2016
Cubs End 108-Year Drought

The Chicago Cubs defeat the Cleveland Indians in Game 7 of the World Series, ending the longest championship drought in professional sports.
