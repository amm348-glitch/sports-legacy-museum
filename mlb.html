MLB
Baseball

America's pastime. Major League Baseball traces its professional roots to 1876 — the oldest major professional sports league in the United States, and a mirror for American history itself.

By the numbers: 30 Teams — 148 Seasons — 162 Games Per Season — $11B Annual Revenue
The Greats

Baseball Legends

The hitters, pitchers, and fielders who wrote the story of America's pastime across 150 years.

Second Base · Brooklyn Dodgers
Jackie Robinson

On April 15, 1947, Robinson broke MLB's color barrier. A 6× All-Star and 1949 batting champion — his courage on and off the field made him the most important figure in baseball history.

Outfield · New York Yankees
Babe Ruth

The Sultan of Swat. Ruth's 60 home runs in 1927 stood as the single-season record for 34 years. His 714 career home runs defined power hitting and made baseball America's dominant sport in the 1920s and 30s.

Center Field · New York Giants
Willie Mays

2× MVP. "The Say Hey Kid" is widely regarded as the greatest all-around baseball player ever. His 1954 World Series catch — known simply as "The Catch" — remains the most celebrated defensive play in baseball history.

Outfield · Atlanta Braves
Hank Aaron

Broke Babe Ruth's all-time home run record on April 8, 1974 with 755 career home runs. Aaron endured death threats throughout his pursuit of Ruth's record, carrying himself with extraordinary dignity.

Shortstop · New York Yankees
Derek Jeter

5× World Series Champion, 14× All-Star. "The Captain" of the Yankees dynasty was the face of baseball in New York for two decades — defined by clutch performances and unwavering professionalism.

Pitcher · New York Yankees
Mariano Rivera

The greatest closer in baseball history. 5× World Series Champion. Rivera's cut fastball was so dominant that hitters knew exactly what was coming — and still couldn't hit it. First unanimous Hall of Fame inductee.

Outfield · Boston Red Sox
Ted Williams

The last player to hit .400 in a season (.406 in 1941). 2× MVP, 2× Triple Crown winner. Williams lost nearly five prime years to military service — making his career statistics all the more remarkable.

Center Field · Los Angeles Angels
Mike Trout

3× MVP, 11× All-Star. Widely considered the best player of his generation. Trout's combination of hitting, fielding, and baserunning is unmatched — his WAR totals through age 29 are the greatest in baseball history.

Center Field · Seattle Mariners
Ken Griffey Jr.

630 career home runs, 10× Gold Glove, 1997 MVP. "The Kid" was the most naturally gifted player of his era — his sweet left-handed swing and effortless grace in center field made him baseball's most beloved star of the 1990s.

Outfield · Pittsburgh Pirates
Roberto Clemente

2× World Series Champion, 1966 MVP, 12× Gold Glove. Clemente died on December 31, 1972, delivering aid to earthquake victims in Nicaragua. MLB retired his number league-wide and named its humanitarian award after him.

Defining Moments

The History of the Game

1876
National League Founded

The first major professional sports league in American history is established with eight charter teams.

1927
Ruth Hits 60 Home Runs

Babe Ruth's Yankees — "Murderers' Row" — post a 110-44 record. Ruth's 60 home runs stood as the record for 34 years.

1947
Jackie Robinson Breaks the Color Line

Robinson's debut with the Brooklyn Dodgers is one of the most significant moments in American sports and civil rights history.

1974
Aaron Breaks Ruth's Record

Hank Aaron hits his 715th home run on April 8, 1974 — breaking Babe Ruth's all-time record amid death threats and hate mail.

2016
Cubs End 108-Year Drought

The Chicago Cubs defeat the Cleveland Indians in Game 7 of the World Series, ending the longest championship drought in professional sports.
