NBA
Basketball

Founded in 1946, the National Basketball Association became the world's premier basketball league — and a global cultural force. From Naismith's peach basket to the global superstar era, this is the story of the game.

By the numbers: 30 Teams — 78 Seasons — 1.3B Global Fans — $10B Annual Revenue
The Greats

NBA Legends

The players who defined eras, broke records, and made the NBA the most watched basketball league on earth.

Shooting Guard · Chicago Bulls
Michael Jordan

6× NBA Champion, 6× Finals MVP, 5× League MVP. The greatest scorer in NBA history at 30.1 PPG. Jordan didn't just win — he redefined what winning looked like.

The NBA's all-time leading scorer. 4× NBA Champion with 3 different franchises. LeBron's longevity and versatility make him the strongest case for the greatest player ever.

Center · Los Angeles Lakers
Kareem Abdul-Jabbar

Former all-time scoring leader with 38,387 points. 6× NBA Champion, 6× MVP. His skyhook shot is the most unstoppable move in basketball history.

Point Guard · Los Angeles Lakers
Magic Johnson

5× NBA Champion, 3× Finals MVP. Magic's arrival in 1979 — alongside Larry Bird — saved a struggling league and sparked the golden era of NBA basketball.

Small Forward · Boston Celtics
Larry Bird

3× NBA Champion, 3× consecutive MVP (1984–1986). Bird's rivalry with Magic Johnson is the defining narrative of 1980s basketball — and one of the greatest rivalries in sports history.

24
Shooting Guard · Los Angeles Lakers
Kobe Bryant

5× NBA Champion, 1× Finals MVP, 1× League MVP. Kobe's Mamba Mentality — the relentless pursuit of excellence — became a cultural philosophy that extended far beyond basketball.

13
Center · Multiple Teams
Wilt Chamberlain

Scored 100 points in a single game on March 2, 1962 — a record that will likely never be broken. Averaged 50.4 PPG for an entire season. The most dominant individual performance in NBA history.

6
Point Guard · Golden State Warriors
Stephen Curry

4× NBA Champion, 2× MVP including unanimous MVP in 2016. Curry revolutionized basketball by proving the three-point shot could be the primary offensive weapon — changing how the game is played at every level.

11
Point Guard · New York Knicks · Brooklyn Nets
Isiah Thomas

2× NBA Champion with the Detroit Pistons "Bad Boys." One of the greatest point guards of all time, Thomas led Detroit to back-to-back championships in 1989 and 1990 with a physical, relentless style.

Dynasty Eras

The Greatest Teams

1984–1998

Chicago Bulls

Michael Jordan's Bulls won 6 championships in 8 years — never losing a Finals series. The 1995–96 team went 72-10 in the regular season, a record that stood for 20 years. Their dominance defined an era.

1980–1988

Los Angeles Lakers — Showtime

Magic Johnson, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, James Worthy. The Showtime Lakers won 5 championships in the 1980s with an up-tempo style that made basketball must-watch television across America.

2014–2019

Golden State Warriors

Steph Curry, Klay Thompson, Draymond Green, and Kevin Durant. Four championships in five years. The Warriors' motion offense and three-point revolution changed how basketball is played at every level.

1956–1969

Boston Celtics — The Original Dynasty

Bill Russell's Celtics won 11 championships in 13 years — including 8 consecutive titles from 1959 to 1966. No team in professional sports history has dominated their era so completely.
