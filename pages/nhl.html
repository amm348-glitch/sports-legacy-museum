American-Born Legends

Hockey Greats

The American-born players who made the NHL their home and helped grow hockey culture across the United States.

Center · Dallas Stars
Mike Modano

1× Stanley Cup Champion. All-time leading scorer among American-born NHL players with 1,374 career points. The face of American hockey for two decades.

Center · Buffalo Sabres / Rangers
Pat LaFontaine

8× All-Star. His 1992-93 season — 53 goals, 95 assists — remains one of the greatest individual seasons by an American in NHL history.

Defense · Multiple Teams
Chris Chelios

3× Stanley Cup Champion, 3× Norris Trophy. Played 26 NHL seasons — the longest career of any American-born player in history.

Defense · New York Rangers
Brian Leetch

1× Stanley Cup Champion, 1994 Conn Smythe Trophy, 2× Norris Trophy. Leetch's brilliance helped end the Rangers' 54-year championship drought in 1994.

Goalie · New York Rangers
Mike Richter

1× Stanley Cup Champion (1994), 1996 World Cup MVP. Named the greatest American-born goalie of all time by Hockey News.

Center · Chicago Blackhawks
Jeremy Roenick

8× All-Star, 513 career goals. One of the most charismatic American players in NHL history — his passion helped grow hockey's profile across the United States.

February 22, 1980

Miracle on Ice

The United States hockey team — a group of college amateurs — defeated the heavily favored Soviet Union 4-3 at the 1980 Winter Olympics in Lake Placid. The Soviets had won the gold medal in 5 of the previous 6 Olympics.

Sports Illustrated named it the greatest sports moment of the 20th century. Al Michaels' call — "Do you believe in miracles? YES!" — remains the most famous line in sports broadcasting history.
