Athletes who transcended their sports. Their stories shaped American culture, challenged injustice, and redefined the limits of human achievement — in and out of the arena.

Individual Sport Athletes

Beyond the Leagues

These athletes competed outside the four major leagues — but their impact on American culture is equal to anyone who ever played.

Boxing · Heavyweight Champion
Muhammad Ali

3× World Heavyweight Champion. Refused the Vietnam draft, stripped of his title, and had his conviction unanimously overturned by the Supreme Court. The most recognized athlete on Earth.

NBA · Chicago Bulls
Michael Jordan

6× NBA Champion. His Air Jordan partnership generates over $5 billion annually — the most successful athlete endorsement in history. Jordan transformed sports marketing forever.

Tennis · WTA
Serena Williams

23 Grand Slam titles. Trained on the public courts of Compton, CA. Williams became an outspoken advocate for racial and gender equality throughout her career.

Golf · PGA Tour
Tiger Woods

15 Major championships, 82 PGA Tour wins. First athlete to reach $1 billion in earnings. His 2019 Masters comeback following four back surgeries is one of the greatest in sporting history.

Gymnastics · USA Gymnastics
Simone Biles

The most decorated American gymnast in history with 37 World and Olympic medals. Has four skills named after her. Her 2020 Olympics mental health decision sparked a global conversation.

Track & Field · 1936 Olympics
Jesse Owens

Won 4 gold medals at the 1936 Berlin Olympics — directly in front of Adolf Hitler. Owens' performance is one of the most powerful moments in the history of sport as a force against racism.

Swimming · Olympics
Michael Phelps

23 Olympic gold medals — the most decorated Olympian in history. Competed in five Olympic Games. His dominance of swimming is unparalleled in Olympic history.

Tennis · WTA
Billie Jean King

39 Grand Slam titles. Defeated Bobby Riggs in the "Battle of the Sexes" before 90 million viewers. Key founder of the WTA and fighter for equal prize money throughout her career.

Cycling · Tour de France
Lance Armstrong

Won 7 consecutive Tour de France titles after surviving cancer. His Livestrong Foundation raised over $500 million for cancer research — leaving a complex but undeniable legacy.

Track & Field · Olympics
Carl Lewis

9 Olympic gold medals across four Games (1984–1996). Named Sportsman of the Century by the International Olympic Committee. Dominated the 100m sprint and long jump for over a decade.

Track & Field · Olympics
Florence Griffith-Joyner

"Flo-Jo." 3 gold medals at the 1988 Seoul Olympics. Her world records in the 100m and 200m have stood for over 35 years — the most enduring records in women's sprinting history.

NFL · MLB · Two-Sport Legend
Bo Jackson

The only athlete ever named All-Star in both the NFL and MLB. Nike's "Bo Knows" campaign became one of the most iconic in sports marketing history.

Football · Baseball · Olympics
Jim Thorpe

Won gold medals in the pentathlon and decathlon at the 1912 Olympics. Named the greatest athlete of the first half of the 20th century by the Associated Press.
