American-Born Legends

Hockey Greats

The American-born players who made the NHL their home and helped grow hockey culture across the United States.

Center · Dallas Stars
Mike Modano

1× Stanley Cup Champion. The all-time leading scorer among American-born NHL players with 1,374 career points. Modano's combination of speed, skill, and longevity made him the face of American hockey for two decades.

Center · Buffalo Sabres / Rangers
Pat LaFontaine

8× All-Star, 1× Masterton Trophy. One of the most gifted American-born players in NHL history. LaFontaine's 1992-93 season — 53 goals, 95 assists — remains one of the greatest individual seasons by an American in NHL history.

Defense · Multiple Teams
Chris Chelios

3× Stanley Cup Champion, 3× Norris Trophy (best defenseman). Chelios played 26 NHL seasons — the longest career of any American-born player in history. His physicality and compete level set the standard for American defensemen.

Defense · New York Rangers
Brian Leetch

1× Stanley Cup Champion, 1994 Conn Smythe Trophy (playoff MVP), 2× Norris Trophy. Leetch's offensive brilliance from the blue line helped end the Rangers' 54-year championship drought in 1994 — one of the most celebrated moments in New York sports history.

Goalie · New York Rangers
Mike Richter

1× Stanley Cup Champion (1994), 1996 World Cup MVP. Richter was the backbone of the Rangers' 1994 championship run — his Game 6 save against Vancouver is one of the most iconic moments in Rangers history. Named the greatest American-born goalie of all time by Hockey News.

Center · Chicago Blackhawks / Multiple Teams
Jeremy Roenick

8× All-Star, 513 career goals. One of the most charismatic and skilled American players in NHL history. Roenick's passion for the game and outspoken personality helped grow hockey's profile in the United States throughout the 1990s and 2000s.

February 22, 1980

Miracle on Ice

In the 1980 Winter Olympics at Lake Placid, the United States hockey team — a group of college amateurs — defeated the heavily favored Soviet Union 4-3 in the medal round. The Soviets had won the gold medal in 5 of the previous 6 Olympics.

Sports Illustrated named it the greatest sports moment of the 20th century. Al Michaels' call — "Do you believe in miracles? YES!" — remains the most famous line in sports broadcasting history.

The US went on to defeat Finland for the gold medal. The game remains the defining moment in American hockey history.

The Trophy

The Stanley Cup

1893 — First Awarded

Lord Stanley's Gift

The Stanley Cup was donated by Lord Stanley of Preston in 1892 and first awarded in 1893. It is the oldest professional sports trophy in North America — predating the World Series by a decade.

Trophy History
The Tradition

A Trophy That Travels

Unlike every other major trophy in professional sports, the Stanley Cup travels. Each player on the winning team gets it for one day during the summer — it has been taken cliff diving, used as a cereal bowl, and brought to the top of mountains.
