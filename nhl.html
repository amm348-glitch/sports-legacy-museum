NHL
Hockey

The fastest game on ice. The National Hockey League has operated since 1917, producing some of the toughest, most relentless athletes in professional sport and cultivating one of the most passionate fanbases in all of sports.

By the numbers: 32 Teams — 107 Seasons — 60 mph Average Puck Speed — $6B Annual Revenue
The Greats

Hockey Legends

The skaters and goalies who defined the ice — from Gretzky's untouchable records to Orr's revolutionary defense.

Center · Edmonton Oilers
Wayne Gretzky

The Great One. 4× Stanley Cup Champion. Holds 61 NHL records — his assists total alone exceeds the total points of every other player in history. Simply the greatest hockey player who ever lived.

4
Defense · Boston Bruins
Bobby Orr

Revolutionized the defenseman position by becoming an offensive force. 2× Stanley Cup Champion, 3× Hart Trophy (MVP). His 1970 overtime goal — captured in the most famous photo in hockey history — won the Bruins their first Cup in 29 years.

9
Right Wing · Montreal Canadiens
Maurice "Rocket" Richard

8× Stanley Cup Champion. The first player to score 50 goals in 50 games (1944-45). Richard's intensity and clutch performances made him a symbol of French-Canadian pride and the most celebrated athlete in Montreal history.

19
Center · Detroit Red Wings
Steve Yzerman

3× Stanley Cup Champion. "Stevie Y" captained the Detroit Red Wings for 20 seasons — the longest captaincy in NHL history. His transformation from pure scorer to defensive stalwart after a knee injury is one of hockey's great stories of reinvention.

30
Goalie · New York Rangers / Vancouver Canucks
Henrik Lundqvist

"The King." 1× Vezina Trophy winner, 2012 Olympic Gold Medal with Sweden. Lundqvist's acrobatic saves and calm under pressure made him the face of the New York Rangers for 15 seasons — and one of the greatest goalies of the modern era.

87
Center · Pittsburgh Penguins
Sidney Crosby

3× Stanley Cup Champion, 2× Conn Smythe Trophy, 2× Hart Trophy. "Sid the Kid" is the defining superstar of his generation — a complete player who combines elite scoring with defensive responsibility and leadership.

The Trophy

The Stanley Cup

1893 — First Awarded

Lord Stanley's Gift

The Stanley Cup was donated by Lord Stanley of Preston, the Governor General of Canada, in 1892. First awarded in 1893 to the Montreal Hockey Club — it is the oldest professional sports trophy in North America, predating the World Series by a decade.

Trophy History
The Tradition

A Trophy That Travels

Unlike every other major trophy in professional sports, the Stanley Cup travels. Each player on the winning team gets it for one day during the summer — taking it home, to their hometown, to hospitals, beaches, and restaurants. It has been taken cliff diving, used as a cereal bowl, and lost by the New Jersey Devils.

Cultural Legacy
February 22, 1980

Miracle on Ice

In the 1980 Winter Olympics at Lake Placid, the United States hockey team — a group of college amateurs — defeated the heavily favored Soviet Union 4-3 in the medal round. The Soviets had won the gold medal in 5 of the previous 6 Olympics.

Sports Illustrated named it the greatest sports moment of the 20th century. Al Michaels' call — "Do you believe in miracles? YES!" — remains the most famous line in sports broadcasting history.

The US went on to defeat Finland for the gold medal. The game remains the defining moment in American hockey history.
