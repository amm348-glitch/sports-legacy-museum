Athletes who transcended their sports. Their stories shaped American culture, challenged injustice, and redefined the limits of human achievement — in and out of the arena.

Individual Sport Athletes

Beyond the Leagues

These athletes competed outside the four major leagues — but their impact on American culture and identity is equal to anyone who ever played.

Boxing · Heavyweight Champion
Muhammad Ali

3× World Heavyweight Champion. Refused the Vietnam draft, was stripped of his title, and had his conviction unanimously overturned by the Supreme Court. The most recognized athlete on Earth — and a symbol of courage far beyond sport.

NBA · Chicago Bulls
Michael Jordan

6× NBA Champion. His Air Jordan partnership with Nike generates over $5 billion annually — the most successful athlete endorsement in history. Jordan didn't just play basketball; he transformed sports marketing forever.

Tennis · WTA
Serena Williams

23 Grand Slam titles — the most in the Open Era. Trained on the public courts of Compton, CA. Williams became an outspoken advocate for racial and gender equality, challenging the sport's governing bodies on pay equity throughout her career.

Golf · PGA Tour
Tiger Woods

15 Major championships, 82 PGA Tour wins. The first athlete to reach $1 billion in earnings. His 2019 Masters victory — following four back surgeries — is considered one of the greatest comebacks in sporting history.

Gymnastics · USA Gymnastics
Simone Biles

The most decorated American gymnast in history with 37 World and Olympic medals. Biles has four skills named after her — a mark of difficulty no other gymnast can execute. Her decision to prioritize mental health at the 2020 Olympics sparked a global conversation.

Track & Field · 1936 Olympics
Jesse Owens

Won 4 gold medals at the 1936 Berlin Olympics — directly in front of Adolf Hitler — in the 100m, 200m, long jump, and 4×100m relay. Owens' performance is one of the most powerful moments in the history of sport as a force against racism.

Swimming · Olympics
Michael Phelps

23 Olympic gold medals — the most decorated Olympian in history. Phelps competed in five Olympic Games and won a medal in every single event he entered across his career. His dominance of swimming is unparalleled in Olympic history.

Tennis · WTA
Billie Jean King

39 Grand Slam titles. Defeated Bobby Riggs in the "Battle of the Sexes" in 1973 before 90 million TV viewers worldwide. King was instrumental in establishing the Women's Tennis Association and fought for equal prize money throughout her career.

Cycling · Tour de France
Lance Armstrong

Won 7 consecutive Tour de France titles (1999–2005) after surviving testicular cancer. Armstrong's comeback story inspired millions — though his titles were later stripped following doping admissions. His Livestrong Foundation raised over $500 million for cancer research.

Track & Field · Olympics
Carl Lewis

9 Olympic gold medals across four Games (1984–1996). Lewis dominated the 100m sprint and long jump for over a decade — his long jump world record stood for 9 years. Named Sportsman of the Century by the International Olympic Committee.

Track & Field · Olympics
Florence Griffith-Joyner

"Flo-Jo." Won 3 gold medals at the 1988 Seoul Olympics. Her world records in the 100m (10.49 sec) and 200m (21.34 sec) have stood for over 35 years — the most enduring records in women's sprinting history. Transformed track and field into must-watch entertainment.

NFL · MLB · Two-Sport Legend
Bo Jackson

The only athlete ever named an All-Star in both the NFL and MLB. Jackson was considered the most physically gifted athlete of his generation — his Nike "Bo Knows" campaign became one of the most iconic in sports marketing history.

Football · Baseball · Olympics
Jim Thorpe

Won gold medals in the pentathlon and decathlon at the 1912 Stockholm Olympics. Also played professional football and baseball. Named the greatest athlete of the first half of the 20th century by the Associated Press — and considered by many the greatest all-around athlete in American history.
