Football

The most-watched sport in America. The National Football League grew from barnstorming regional teams into an $18 billion annual enterprise that dominates American media, culture, and Sunday afternoons.

By the numbers: 32 Teams — 100M+ Super Bowl Viewers — $18B Annual Revenue — 57 Super Bowls
The Greats

NFL Legends

The quarterbacks, receivers, and defenders who defined football across the decades.

Quarterback · Patriots / Buccaneers
Tom Brady

7× Super Bowl Champion — more than any franchise in NFL history. Brady's 23-year career produced records for passing yards, touchdowns, and sustained excellence that may never be matched.

16
Quarterback · San Francisco 49ers
Joe Montana

4× Super Bowl Champion, never threw an interception in a Super Bowl. Montana's composure under pressure earned him the nickname "Joe Cool." Many consider him the greatest quarterback of all time.

4
Quarterback · Green Bay Packers
Brett Favre

1× Super Bowl Champion, 3× consecutive MVP (1995–1997). Favre's iron man streak of 297 consecutive starts remains a testament to durability and competitive fire. The ultimate gunslinger.

88
Wide Receiver · Dallas Cowboys
Michael Irvin

3× Super Bowl Champion with the Dallas Cowboys dynasty of the 1990s. "The Playmaker" was one of the most physically dominant receivers of his era, combining size, speed, and an unmatched competitive intensity.

56
Linebacker · Chicago Bears
Lawrence Taylor

The most disruptive defensive player in NFL history. Taylor's 1986 season — 20.5 sacks — is considered the greatest defensive performance ever. He changed how offenses designed blocking schemes.

80
Wide Receiver · San Francisco 49ers
Jerry Rice

The greatest wide receiver of all time — 3× Super Bowl Champion, 1× Super Bowl MVP. Rice's 197 receiving touchdowns are an NFL record by a margin so large it may never be challenged.

34
Running Back · Chicago Bears
Walter Payton

"Sweetness." 1× Super Bowl Champion, 1× MVP. Payton's 16,726 career rushing yards stood as the all-time record for 18 years. His toughness, grace, and work ethic made him the model of a complete football player.

19
Quarterback · Indianapolis Colts
Peyton Manning

2× Super Bowl Champion, 5× MVP — the most in NFL history. Manning's pre-snap intelligence revolutionized the quarterback position. His mastery of the audible made him the game's premier field general.

32
Safety · Pittsburgh Steelers
Ronnie Lott

4× Super Bowl Champion. Considered the most feared defensive back in NFL history. Lott's physicality set a standard for the safety position — he famously had the tip of his finger amputated rather than miss the 1986 season.

Iconic Super Bowls

Games That Defined an Era

I

Packers 35, Chiefs 10

January 15, 1967. Tickets cost $6. The first Super Bowl drew 61,946 fans to the LA Memorial Coliseum — and was not sold out. Today, a Super Bowl ticket averages $10,000.

XLII

Giants 17, Patriots 14

February 3, 2008. David Tyree's helmet catch. The New York Giants ended the Patriots' quest for a perfect 19-0 season in one of the greatest upsets in Super Bowl history.

LI

Patriots 34, Falcons 28 (OT)

February 5, 2017. Tom Brady orchestrated the greatest comeback in Super Bowl history — erasing a 28-3 deficit in the fourth quarter to win in the first-ever Super Bowl overtime.

III

Jets 16, Colts 7

January 12, 1969. Joe Namath guaranteed a victory — then delivered. The AFL's triumph over the NFL establishment validated the merger and changed professional football forever.

XXIII

49ers 20, Bengals 16

January 22, 1989. Joe Montana, 92 yards, 3:10 remaining. The greatest two-minute drill in Super Bowl history ended with a touchdown pass to John Taylor. Montana's poise under pressure at its finest.

XLIX

Patriots 28, Seahawks 24

February 1, 2015. Malcolm Butler's goal-line interception with 26 seconds remaining. Perhaps the most shocking final play in Super Bowl history — Seattle called a pass instead of running Marshawn Lynch.
