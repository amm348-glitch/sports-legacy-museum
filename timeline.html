The complete timeline of American sports history — from the founding of the National League in 1876 to the present day. Filter by sport to trace your own story through the collection.

Filter by Sport
1876
MLB
National League Founded

The National League of Professional Base Ball Clubs is established — the first major professional sports league in American history. Eight charter teams compete for a pennant, establishing the model for professional sports organization in America.

League History
1903
MLB
First World Series

The Boston Americans defeat the Pittsburgh Pirates in the first modern World Series, 5 games to 3. The championship series between the American and National Leagues becomes the defining event of baseball's year.

Championship
1920
NFL
NFL Founded

The American Professional Football Association is founded in Canton, Ohio with 14 teams. Renamed the National Football League in 1922, it begins its journey from regional curiosity to America's most watched sport.

League History
1927
MLB
Babe Ruth Hits 60 Home Runs

Ruth's New York Yankees — "Murderers' Row" — post a 110-44 record. Ruth's 60 home run season stands as the record for 34 years. The Sultan of Swat makes baseball America's dominant sport.

Record
1942
NHL
Original Six Era Begins

The NHL settles into its "Original Six" format — Boston Bruins, Chicago Black Hawks, Detroit Red Wings, Montreal Canadiens, New York Rangers, and Toronto Maple Leafs — that will define the league for 25 years.

League History
1947
MLB · Civil Rights
Jackie Robinson Breaks the Color Line

On April 15, 1947, Jackie Robinson takes the field for the Brooklyn Dodgers — becoming the first African American to play in MLB in the modern era. His courage under immense pressure helps crack open America's conscience on racial equality.

Cultural Milestone
1946
NBA
BAA Founded — NBA Begins

The Basketball Association of America is founded by arena owners. It merges with the rival NBL in 1949 to become the NBA. Professional basketball begins its long ascent to global dominance.

League History
1958
NFL
The Greatest Game Ever Played

The Baltimore Colts defeat the New York Giants 23-17 in sudden death overtime — the first overtime game in NFL history. Broadcast nationally on television, the game introduces the country to professional football and launches the NFL's rise to dominance.

Landmark Game
1967
NFL
First Super Bowl

Super Bowl I: Green Bay Packers 35, Kansas City Chiefs 10. Tickets cost $6–$12. The game is not sold out. Today, Super Bowl tickets average $10,000 and the event is watched by over 100 million Americans.

Championship
1967
Culture
Muhammad Ali Refuses the Draft

Ali refuses military induction, citing religious beliefs and opposition to the Vietnam War. He is stripped of his heavyweight title and banned from boxing for three years. The Supreme Court unanimously overturns his conviction in 1971.

Cultural Milestone
1972
Culture
Title IX Passed

Title IX of the Education Amendments prohibits sex discrimination in federally funded education programs. The legislation transforms women's participation in American sports at every level — from high school to the Olympics.

Legislation
1980
NHL · Olympics
Miracle on Ice

Team USA defeats the Soviet Union 4-3 at the Lake Placid Winter Olympics. Sports Illustrated names it the greatest sports moment of the 20th century. Al Michaels: "Do you believe in miracles? YES!"

Cultural Milestone
1984
NBA
Michael Jordan Drafted

The Chicago Bulls select Michael Jordan with the 3rd pick in the 1984 NBA Draft. Over the next 15 years, Jordan transforms basketball into a global phenomenon — and the NBA into a $10 billion enterprise.

Draft
1985
NBA
Air Jordan 1 Released

Nike releases the Air Jordan 1. The NBA fines Jordan $5,000 per game for wearing it — Nike pays the fines and turns the controversy into an ad campaign. The shoe generates $162 million in its first year and permanently changes sports marketing.

Culture
1994
MLB
Baseball Strike Cancels World Series

Players strike on August 12, 1994 — canceling the World Series for the first time since 1904. The work stoppage devastates fan attendance and trust, beginning baseball's long road back to relevance.

Labor History
1998
NBA
Jordan's Last Shot

Michael Jordan hits the series-winning jumper over Bryon Russell with 5.2 seconds remaining in Game 6 of the NBA Finals against the Utah Jazz — his sixth and final championship. The image becomes one of the most iconic in sports photography.

Defining Moment
2004
Culture
LeBron James Enters the NBA

Selected 1st overall by the Cleveland Cavaliers, LeBron James begins the career that will eventually see him surpass Michael Jordan as the NBA's all-time leading scorer and reignite the greatest debate in sports history.

Draft
2016
MLB
Cubs End 108-Year Championship Drought

The Chicago Cubs defeat the Cleveland Indians in Game 7 of the World Series — ending the longest championship drought in professional sports history. Over 40 million viewers tune in for Game 7.

Championship
2016
Culture
Colin Kaepernick Takes a Knee

San Francisco 49ers quarterback Colin Kaepernick kneels during the national anthem to protest police brutality and racial injustice. The gesture sparks a national conversation about the role of athletes in social justice that continues today.

Cultural Milestone
2023
NBA
LeBron James Becomes All-Time Scoring Leader

LeBron James surpasses Kareem Abdul-Jabbar's all-time NBA scoring record of 38,387 points on February 7, 2023 — in front of a sold-out crowd in Los Angeles. The moment ends a debate that defined a generation of basketball.
